# Reviewer labelling guide (DRAFT)

Status: draft for the lecturer. Reviewers label anonymised profiles only after ethics approval. Reviewers must not see any output of the recommendation system.

## Your task

For each anonymised student profile, judge how suitable each career on the list is for that student, now or as a realistic next step.

## Materials you receive

- The anonymised profiles, one per page.
- The career list for each profile, in alphabetical order, each with a link to its O*NET page. The list contains all careers proposed by any method plus your own additions; it does not show which method proposed which career, and it shows no scores.
- A blank grade sheet.

## Grades

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 2 | Good fit: the student's skills cover the core daily work of the career, or the career is a realistic next step in a direction the student states |
| 1 | Plausible: suitable after significant additional training |
| 0 | Not suitable on the information given |

Careers not on your list count as 0 unless you add them. You may add up to 3 careers from the full catalogue that you think are missing.

## Rules

1. Judge what the occupation actually does (use the O*NET link if unsure), not how many words overlap with the profile.
2. Generic skills alone (communication, Excel, Git) do not make a career a good fit.
3. Education alone never makes a career unsuitable; note concerns in the comment column.
4. Interests support a judgement but do not create a fit on their own.
5. Do not discuss profiles with other reviewers until you have submitted your grades.
6. If a profile is too vague to judge, mark it "insufficient information" and grade nothing.

## Calibration

Before the real profiles, grade the 5 practice profiles, then meet with the other reviewers to discuss disagreements. Practice profiles are not used in the results.

| Profile code | Career id | Career title | Grade (0, 1, 2) | Comment (optional) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
