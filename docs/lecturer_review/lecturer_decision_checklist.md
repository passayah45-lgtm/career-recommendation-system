Where the second review recommends an option, that is marked as its recommendation. The decisions themselves are the lecturer's.

## Decision 6. Approval of the interest selection limit

**What to review:** the selection-limit section of the second review, and section 9 of the first audit.

**Why it matters:** today a student can select every interest. Every career then gets the full interest score, and interests stop affecting the ranking. A limit closes that loophole without changing the formula. A strict limit forces students with many interests to leave some out. The limit has never been tested: no synthetic profile uses more than 4 interests.

**Options:**
- A. A soft warning above 5 interests, then a separate experiment before any hard cap (second review's recommendation).
- B. A hard cap of 5 interests (the first audit's recommendation).
- C. No limit.
- D. A different number, to be tested.

The second review recommends a separate experiment before any hard cap, whichever option is chosen.

## Decision 7. Approval of the O*NET validation approach

**What to review:** `riasec_classification_review.md` (section 4) and section E of the second review: the sources, how codes were read, and the method limits.

**Why it matters:** O*NET is the only independent check on the assignments. The codes were read from list pages through a web tool, with letters only and no scores. O*NET describes US occupations.

**Options:**
- A. Accept the list-page evidence as it is, with its limits stated.
- B. Spot-check a sample of codes against the O*NET database files before implementation (suggested in the second review's risk table).
- C. Option B, and also look for African or national occupational sources.
- D. Use O*NET only as a consistency check, and rely on lecturer or specialist review for the final assignments.
