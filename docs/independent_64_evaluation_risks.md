# Independent 64-career evaluation: risks report (eval64_v1)

Written 1 November 2026, updated 2 November 2026 after the review tasks. Unresolved issues are listed, not hidden.

| # | Risk | Severity | Evidence | Mitigation in place | Unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Synthetic profiles do not represent all real learners | High | 111 invented profiles; 61 are strong matches; no real input data | Profile types cover hard cases (French, career change, lower education, interests only, no clear fit); no personal data used | Real anonymised profiles need ethics approval (`research/experimental_protocol.md`) |
| 2 | Author bias in labels | High | One labeller (an AI assistant) wrote all 363 labels, and also wrote the 14 career records added in v1.3.0 and their skill lists | Rules fixed before labelling; labels frozen before any engine run; justification and confidence for every label; blank shuffled reviewer sheet | No second reviewer yet, so no agreement statistic. Results must be cited as single-author |
| 3 | Labeller saw the catalogue | Medium | The labeller knew the careers' skill lists, so labels and engine may share assumptions | Grades are based on occupational work, not on skill overlap (rule R1); profile skills written in free vocabulary | Blind labelling by a reviewer who has not seen the catalogue |
| 4 | Vocabulary favours catalogue-defined profiles | High | 269 of 480 skill entries (56.0%) unrecognised; 22 of 108 profiles with skills have none recognised (verified through the engine's parser); shares run from 22.4% (Data and AI profiles) to 76.1% (Design); frequent misses include report writing, AutoCAD, interviewing | One definition and denominator (`vocabulary_analysis.csv`); the withdrawn subgroup figure no longer appears | Alias additions must be tested on a new profile set, because these profiles motivated them |
| 5 | Exposure imbalance between careers | Medium | Data and AI careers take 22.7% of top-5 slots against 9.9% of profiles; 97 of their 126 slots go to other categories' profiles and 92 are grade 0; Business Intelligence Analyst appears 26 times, 3 relevant, 21 grade 0, 14 with no matched skill | Causes identified: interests let low-scoring careers in, and career-ID ties then favour C001 to C008. The exploratory sensitivity analysis shows Data and AI slots fall from 126 to about 81 to 83 without the ID preference; list-length normalisation barely changes them (22.7% to 22.5 or 24.1%). Formula unchanged | Real-usage monitoring; any change to the formula belongs in a separate experiment |
| 6 | Limited public occupational data for some careers | Medium | 12 of 64 careers have no O*NET code, including 9 of the new ones; labels for them rely on general occupational knowledge | Each label names its basis | National (Guinean, ECOWAS) occupational references |
| 7 | Education levels are partly estimates | Medium | All 14 new levels are estimates (5 agree with a US reference); education weight is 0.05 | Education effect measured: relevant careers above the learner's level still rank in the top 5 for 91% of 22 pairs | The engine's ranking does not signal entry requirements strongly |
| 8 | Resources differ in quality, access and licensing | Low for this study | 30 new resources not yet HTTP-checked by the project checker; some non-commercial licences | Not used by any metric in this study; documented in `docs/african_expansion_link_check.md` | Link check from a normal network |
| 9 | Original and expanded evaluations are separate studies | High if misread | Different profiles, labelling, granularity and catalogue size | Reported side by side with explicit caveats; original reports unchanged and fingerprint-tested | A controlled comparison is designed but not run |
| 10 | Performance does not measure employment outcomes | High if misread | Metrics measure agreement with labels | Stated in every summary, report and chart subtitle | Outcome data would need a longitudinal study |
| 11 | Recommendations do not establish suitability for regulated professions | High if misread | Nurse, pharmacist, physical therapist and lawyer need licensing; law needs national admission | Rule R5 keeps licensed professions at grade 1 or below without matching training | The app text should keep stating that it does not assess eligibility |
| 12 | Small samples per category and per career | Medium | About 10 profiles per category; 35 careers have fewer than 3 relevant labels | Category intervals reported as indicative; per-career recall marked "too few labels" | More profiles per career |
| 13 | Ties decided by career ID | High | All 62 rank-5 ties are decided by career ID (the earlier sort keys never separate them); 139 of 555 top-5 slots (25.0%) are decided inside career-ID ties; original careers win 79 such pairs near the top 5 against new careers, never the reverse; 10 relevant original careers lose their top-5 place on ID; no relevant new career does | Measured with the engine's own sort key (section 16.4). Exploratory test: a hash or seeded random last key changes 54 profiles' top 5 and about 210 of 555 positions, cuts Data and AI slots from 126 to about 81, adds about 7 new-career slots, and changes no metric measurably (`docs/independent_64_sensitivity_analysis.md`). Ranking unchanged | A pre-registered tie-break evaluation on new profiles before any change |
| 14 | Unbalanced profile types | Medium | 61 of 111 profiles are strong matches, a consequence of writing one clear profile per career for coverage; only 2 of 12 types reach 10 profiles; 7 types have fewer than 5 | All 12 types reported with sample status; no profiles added to change results | A balanced profile mix, designed in advance, in eval64_v2 |
| 15 | Profile set reused for future tuning | Medium | Weak profiles point directly to aliases that would raise scores | This report forbids tuning on eval64_v1; a correction or tuning study needs eval64_v2 | Governance relies on the project author |
| 16 | Technical reproducibility | Low | Bootstrap seed and deterministic engine; `--check` reproduces all outputs | Frozen files with SHA-256; tests check integrity | Package upgrades may change floating-point rounding; `--check` will show it |
| 17 | Interest scoring depends on list length | Low | A career with 3 interests earns 8.3 points per match and one with 4 earns 6.25; 252 of 555 slots (45.4%) are earned with no matched skill | Exploratory test: list-length-neutral scoring (fixed unit, cosine) changes 5 to 27 profiles, metrics by 0.011 or less, and the Data and AI share by under 1.5 points; removing interests lowers NDCG@5 from 0.759 to 0.560. Interests matter, their normalisation does not | None beyond monitoring; formula unchanged |
| 18 | Second review not yet done | High | No reviewer data | Blank shuffled sheet, readable packet, guide, and an analysis that refuses incomplete sheets | A real reviewer, then the agreement analysis |
| 19 | Review-stage analyses were designed after seeing results | Low | Sections 16.1 to 16.4 | Labelled as explanatory, not pre-registered; frozen data unchanged | Pre-register diagnostics in eval64_v2 |
| 20 | Post-hoc sensitivity results could be read as a recommendation to deploy | Medium | Several alternatives and metrics compared after seeing the data on single-author labels | Labelled exploratory and post-hoc; no significance claims; nothing deployed; production code untouched (tests check the replica and the frozen files) | Pre-registered evaluation of any change on eval64_v2 with second-review labels |
| 21 | Enriched review sample misread as representative | High | Listed pairs are 5.1% of all 7,104 pairs but 62% of the 585 reviewed; unequal, known inclusion probabilities (1 or about 1 in 30) | Design documented; analysis reports listed-stratum kappa, unlisted-stratum estimates and design-weighted all-pair estimates separately, each with its population; unweighted 585-pair kappa labelled as sample-only | Wide intervals for the unlisted and all-pair estimates (222 sampled pairs); more unlisted draws would need a new sheet |
| 22 | Residual anonymisation hints | Low | Career titles must stay intact; "Management Information Systems Specialist (MIAGE)" and some profile texts (Conakry, Boke) hint at the African expansion; obviously unrelated careers hint at the random stratum | IDs, order and O*NET column removed; codes from a system random source; mapping outside the bundle | None without changing titles, which is not allowed |
| 23 | Reviewer could find the labels in the public repository | Low | The repository is public on GitHub | The labels file, every per-pair label output and the private key are git-ignored and not published (results report section 18); per-profile label examples are redacted from the documents; the handover forbids opening the repository; signed declaration | The published profiles file still shows each profile's type (for example no_clear_fit), a weak hint about expected relevance; labels to be published after the review |

## Integrity of the original study

- `data/evaluation/catalog_v1.2.0/`, `data/evaluation/labelled_profiles.json` and the five saved reports are unchanged. Their SHA-256 values are checked by tests.
- This study writes only to `data/evaluation/catalog_v1.3.0/`, `reports/evaluation_64/` and new files in `src/evaluation/`, `tests/` and `docs/`.
